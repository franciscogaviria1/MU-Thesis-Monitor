# Business Thesis

## Purpose

The MU thesis is that Micron can benefit from a sustained memory-cycle recovery amplified by AI infrastructure investment. The thesis should strengthen only when current, sourced evidence shows improving demand, pricing, supply discipline, and business outcomes.

## Thesis Drivers

### AI Infrastructure Growth

AI training and inference require memory alongside compute. Growth in data-center accelerators, servers, and related infrastructure can increase demand for advanced DRAM and storage products. This matters because durable infrastructure spending can expand Micron's addressable market beyond a conventional memory-cycle recovery.

### HBM Demand

High-bandwidth memory (HBM) is required by many AI accelerators. Strong HBM demand can improve Micron's product mix, capacity utilization, and pricing power. It also matters indirectly: HBM consumes more manufacturing capacity than conventional DRAM, which may tighten supply across other DRAM categories.

### DRAM Pricing

DRAM is a major driver of Micron's revenue and profitability. Rising or stable contract and spot prices may indicate healthier supply-demand conditions and stronger margins. Falling prices, especially alongside inventory growth, may indicate weakening demand or excess supply.

### NAND Pricing

NAND pricing affects Micron's storage businesses and overall earnings sensitivity to the memory cycle. Sustainable price improvement can support revenue and margins, while renewed price declines may offset strength elsewhere.

### Memory Supply and Demand

Memory markets are cyclical and sensitive to capital spending, production growth, inventory, and customer demand. The thesis requires demand growth and industry supply discipline to remain reasonably balanced. Capacity additions, technology transitions, competitor behavior, and customer inventory must therefore be monitored together rather than in isolation.

## Why These Factors Matter

The thesis is strongest when the drivers reinforce one another:

- AI infrastructure growth creates incremental memory demand.
- HBM adoption improves mix and absorbs manufacturing capacity.
- DRAM and NAND pricing confirm broad market health.
- Supply discipline prevents new capacity from overwhelming demand.
- The combined effect produces improving margins, cash generation, and durable earnings power.

No single headline or metric proves the thesis. Evidence must be evaluated over time, across independent sources, and against management guidance and reported results.

## What Evidence Would Invalidate This Thesis?

The thesis should be considered materially weakened or invalidated if sustained evidence shows one or more of the following:

- AI infrastructure spending slows without another source of memory demand replacing it.
- HBM demand, qualification progress, market share, or realized economics fall materially below expectations.
- DRAM or NAND prices decline persistently because supply growth exceeds end demand.
- Industry capital spending or capacity expansion causes prolonged oversupply.
- Customer inventories rise while sell-through and end-market demand weaken.
- Micron's margins, cash generation, or execution fail to improve despite favorable industry conditions.
- Competitors establish a durable technology, cost, yield, or customer advantage that Micron cannot close.
- The expected benefits are already reflected in the share price while business evidence no longer supports further improvement.

Invalidation requires corroborated evidence, not a single adverse data point. Temporary volatility should be distinguished from a persistent change in the thesis.
